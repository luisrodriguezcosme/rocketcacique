Three days after Jesus died, two of his followers walked home from Jerusalem to a village called Emmaus, about seven miles away (Luke 24:13-35). They were sad and confused. Their hopes had died with him.

On the road, Jesus came and walked with them, but they didn't know it was him. He asked what they were talking about, and he listened. Then he walked them through what the Scriptures said.

I've been building a Bible study app named after that walk. It's called [Seven Miles](https://walksevenmiles.com), or Siete Millas in Spanish. It's free, it's early, and it's a personal project. It doesn't come from a church or an organization.

## Who it's for

Many people I care about have been hurt by church. They were judged, controlled, and told what to do, sometimes with Bible verses used as weapons. Many of them stopped trusting Christians, and I understand why.

I don't think the Bible is only for scholars and pastors. Some of Jesus' first followers were fishermen. Seven Miles is a place to read it for yourself, in context, and to bring your real questions without being judged for asking.

## What's in it

- **The Bible**, in English and Spanish, side by side if you want. Tap a word to see what it means and the Hebrew or Greek behind it. Search and cross references, with no sign-up.
- **Hard questions.** Answers to the questions people carry, such as "Does Proverbs 22:6 promise that a child raised in church will come back?" Claude helped write each one. I checked it against sources and approved it.
- **Life paths.** Short readings on anxiety, forgiveness, grief, shame, anger, money, and purpose. They're the one place I share where I land. Each one ends with my own view, marked as mine, next to the main view other Christians hold.
- **A guide** you can ask questions in your own words. For now it's open only to a few testers.

Every answer is labeled so you can tell what you're reading: **The text** (what the words say, in context), **Christians agree**, **Christians disagree** (the main views and their reasons, with no winner picked), **Not in the text** (a saying that isn't in the Bible, or a verse pulled out of context), and **Background** (history, language, and what scholars think).

That fourth label gets a lot of use. "God won't give you more than you can handle" sounds like a verse. The closest one, 1 Corinthians 10:13, is about temptation, not hard times.

## The rule I care about most

For people who have had verses used against them, a misquote isn't a small bug. It's the exact harm the app is meant to undo.

So the guide never types a Bible verse from memory. Every verse comes from real Bible texts in the app's own library, and you can tap any verse to read it in context. After the guide answers, a second pass checks each citation against the verses it points to, and fixes or drops anything they don't support.

I test this with hard questions in both languages, 200 answers per full run. The early misquotes surprised me. They weren't made-up verses. They were small, confident slips. Martha crying, when John 11:33 says it was Mary and the others. Jacob giving a tenth, when Genesis 28:22 is only his promise to. A guess about what Psalm 137's singers had seen, stated as if the psalm said it.

The fix for that last kind was a rule: a guess goes under Background and says who holds it, such as "many scholars think." It never goes under The text. Over a few days of fixes, misquotes went from about 13% to 1%: 2 answers out of 200.

## Kind isn't the same as true

Then I hit a second limit. Seven Miles has to cost nothing to run, so the live guide uses Google's free Gemini model instead.

On the same 200 answers, the free model had 37 misquote flags. I read every one. Most were loose citations. Two I called harmful, and they had something in common. Neither one made a verse harsher. Both made a hard verse softer.

One said the rod in Proverbs 23:13-14 is a shepherd's guiding tool, not something meant to hurt, and cited the passage. The passage talks about striking a child. The other said Malachi 2 shows God's sorrow over broken marriages. The weeping in that chapter is the people's. In verse 16, depending on the translation, God either says he hates divorce or condemns the man who divorces his wife. Neither one is sorrow.

Both answers were trying to be gentle. Both changed what the text says. For someone who has been hurt by these exact verses, a softer version isn't comfort. It's one more person telling them what the Bible says, instead of letting them read it. Seven Miles shows hard verses in full and explains them honestly. It won't soften them, and it won't use them against you. Both halves of that promise matter.

A better prompt barely moved the numbers. What helped was letting the model think longer before it answers. On a rerun of the 78 hardest questions, the harmful answers were gone, though some loose ones stayed. So the guide stays with a few testers for now, with a daily limit, and the reviewed answers in Hard questions carry the depth. It's a small, free model doing a hard job, and now I know where it slips.

## Care in small places

Some choices don't show up in a feature list:

- No account, no ads, no tracking. Notes, highlights, and past chats stay in your own browser. The server keeps no chats.
- The guide isn't private, because what you type goes to Google. The app says so in plain words before your first question.
- If something is too personal for that, there's a **This is personal** button. It shows real places to get help and sends nothing.
- If you write about hurting yourself or being hurt, the app doesn't send it to the AI. It points you to real people instead.
## How it gets built

I have about two hours a week for this. Claude writes most of the code and drafts most of the documents. My hours go to the parts I can't hand off: decisions, theology, tone, and reading answers until I trust them. It's months ahead of the plan I wrote in October, and that split is the reason.

## Try it, then tell me

[Open Seven Miles](https://walksevenmiles.com) on your phone or computer. The Bible, Hard questions, and Life paths work right away. The guide is still with testers, and the name may still change.

I'm looking for feedback from people who have been hurt by church, and from the people who love them. Did anything feel preachy or judgmental? Did you learn something true? Would you come back? Send me a message on LinkedIn.

On the road to Emmaus, Jesus asked a question and listened before he explained anything. That's the order I'm trying to keep. Seven Miles is meant to walk with you, not push you. You decide for yourself.
